Cohort 03 · The program

Eight weeks, one robot, one layer at a time

One 3-hour session a week, in person in Menlo Park. Each week adds a new field of engineering to the same machine, so nothing is learned in isolation.

Week by week

The tags on the right show which fields of engineering are in play that week. They only ever add up.

WEEK 1

Robot architecture, lab safety & your first limb

Understand the robot as a system before touching a screw: degrees of freedom, and the loop between actuators, sensors, controller, and battery. Start an engineering notebook. Then build one limb, which teaches joints, servo orientation, fasteners, and alignment.

MechanicalTeam
WEEK 2

Arms & legs

Split into sub-teams to build the remaining limbs. Cable routing, bearings, torque limits, and checking every joint moves freely before moving on.

MechanicalTeam
WEEK 3

Torso, head & neck

Integrate the limbs onto the torso, then install the head, neck, and stereo cameras. Finish with a mechanical inspection checklist.

MechanicalTeam
WEEK 4

Electronics & wiring checks

Power system, onboard computer, servo bus, IMU, connector labeling, and multimeter basics. Every connection is checked before power-on.

Hard lesson: bad wiring beats good code every time.
MechanicalElectricalTeam
WEEK 5

Safe power-on, servo IDs & calibration

Follow the power-on checklist, assign servo IDs, and zero-point calibrate every joint.

Milestone: the robot powers on safely, and every joint is recognized and calibrated.
MechanicalElectricalSoftwareTeam
WEEK 6

Joint scripts & logging

Linux, the Python environment, and robot config files. The practical task: move joint 3 by 10 degrees, log the commanded vs. actual angle, and explain the difference. A real robotics lesson, not AI magic.

MechanicalElectricalSoftwareTeam
WEEK 7

Keyframe motion & MuJoCo intro

Open-loop vs. closed-loop control. Design a simple standing or keyframe motion and run it on the robot. Load the robot’s model in MuJoCo and compare one motion in simulation and on hardware.

MechanicalElectricalSoftwareSimulationTeam
WEEK 8

Reinforcement learning preview & showcase

What a policy, reward, observation, and action are. Watch a supplied policy train in simulation and change one reward. Then the showcase: a demo of your robot’s verified motion and a short team presentation, including what didn’t work and why.

Milestone: the showcase.

Inside a session

Three hours, mostly hands on the robot

Every session follows the same rhythm, so students spend their energy on the robot, not on figuring out what to do.

Start

A short primer

Just enough theory to know what you’re about to try, and what to measure.

Most of the time

Hands on

Build, wire, run, break, fix. Teammates rotate between operating the robot and checking each other’s work.

End

Debrief

What did you predict? What actually happened? Why? It all goes in the engineering notebook.

How we teach

Debugging isn’t a detour. It’s the curriculum.

Our approach is described in a peer-reviewed paper accepted at IEEE TALE 2026. Read it ↗

01

One robot connects every subject

When a standing attempt fails, students explain it using both balance and control. Mechanical, electrical, and software work all show up in the same behavior.

02

Big tasks, checkable steps

One actuator, one limb, the lower body, the full robot. Each has an explicit success check: safety induction, component checks, power-on, calibration, first motion.

03

Predict in simulation, check on hardware

Students make predictions in MuJoCo, then measure the real robot, and explain the difference.

04

Worked example → one change → your call

Start from working code, change one thing, then make bounded decisions of your own, like designing your team’s keyframe motion.

05

Team robot, individual understanding

Four students share one robot, but each keeps their own notebook of predictions, measurements, and failure explanations.
